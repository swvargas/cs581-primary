## Replace “What to do if something looks wrong” with this response sequence

1. **Establish incident control.** Notify the shift supervisor, physical-security lead, and cyber incident lead. Record discovery, the observed protection impairment, affected portal, and known override interval. Assign a named incident coordinator and NRC-notification owner. Initiate the notification assessment in parallel with containment; neither actor attribution nor final material reconciliation is a prerequisite to escalating the confirmed impairment.
2. **Protect the affected area.** Physical security must verify portal, adjacent access paths, surveillance coverage, and emergency egress. Establish approved guarding/escort and independent observation measures where required. Operations and material-control leads decide how to secure or suspend the affected inventory/transfer activity. Do not infer that the area is secure from register reversion or a normal dashboard alone.
3. **Preserve evidence alongside urgent protective actions.** Capture available controller/API journals, register/configuration state, token-cache metadata and revocation/sync history, relevant firewall/connector records, SIEM exports, and camera/reader evidence. Seek preservation before ring-buffer overwrite. Record collector, source, acquisition time, source time/timezone, collection method, hashes, and access-controlled storage location. Preserve sensitive token data through the incident team's approved evidence process; keep secrets out of chat and GitHub. Do not delay an urgent protective action merely to complete collection; document resulting evidence loss.
4. **Contain the access path.** The authorized network/connector owner should restrict the offending response connection and sessions using a validated change plan. Identify required monitoring flows first; preserve needed telemetry and a trusted recovery route. The IAM and PSI owners must invalidate the local cached credential as well as the corporate identity, and address active sessions. Do not treat ticket closure or SSH-key cleanup as proof that all credential mechanisms were revoked.
5. **Restore the protection function through a validated procedure.** A qualified operator must verify the correct asset/point mapping, remove unauthorized bypass and camera-inhibit settings through its supported interface, and check actual portal and camera-trigger behavior under controlled conditions. Coordinate with physical security and operations before restarting services or changing door/barrier state. The inherited blanket “return to safe resting state” instruction must account for occupancy, emergency egress, and required access; it is not an automatic instruction to lock every portal.
6. **Resolve cross-system consequences.** Request connector/boundary evidence from ICT-SIEM/network owners, independent temperature/level/flow and control-change evidence from SFPCM, and EDG functional checks from its owner where indicated. Material control must reconcile physical inventory, authorized transfers, and accounting records. Track unknowns separately from negative findings. Fourteen OSDP supervision frames do not prove a badge presentation, entry, or absence of entry.
7. **Verify and hand back.** Keep compensatory measures until the responsible leads accept the recovery evidence below. Record each change, verification result, unresolved limitation, responsible owner, and next review. Maintain a shift-to-shift briefing until closure. Report new significant findings through the designated notification owner using the classification decision record.

## Implementation and rollback conditions

Before an authorized implementation, the responsible owner must record the tested configuration baseline, required telemetry and emergency-access dependencies, approved change scope, recovery path, and the person empowered to stop the change. Stop if the change impairs emergency egress, required monitoring, or an assessed safety function. Use the approved recovery procedure and compensatory protection; do not automatically re-enable a compromised credential or broad connector rule to regain service. Resume only after the affected operational/security leads accept the revised plan.

If the platform cannot enforce approval, expiry, or durable audit export, document that capability gap and retain an approved manual control or compensatory measure pending engineering work. The five-minute, sixty-second, and thirty-day targets above are proposed design targets, not demonstrated platform capabilities or legal retention periods. Automatic override expiry must be engineered and tested for occupancy and egress consequences.

Completion of this assignment means documenting the control changes and their acceptance criteria. It does not require running these procedures. Evidence gaps can remain open in the case record while this proposed runbook update is complete; an operational recovery declaration would require the separate acceptance evidence above.
