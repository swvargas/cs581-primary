# Technical Analysis

This analysis reconstructs the fictional **ICT-SIEM -> RMS -> EDGC** campaign from the Evidence Appendix, using W1-W5 as explicitly bounded antecedents. It is a written assessment, not a new plant investigation. Repository paths below are root-relative; source line numbers refer to the cited input files. The working evidence ledger is preserved in `workshops/midterm/session-log.md`, under "Evidence Ledger".

**Evidence labels:** **Stipulated** means an assignment fact; **recorded (scenario)** means an embedded fictional journal/SIEM/register excerpt, not a collection performed here; **recorded (workshop)** means preserved historical workshop evidence; **owner-reported** means a statement relayed through an owner/user; **inferred** means an analytical link; **unknown** means not established. A historical source's "documented" TTP does not turn its hypothetical nuclear application into an observation.

An outward logging session, even a persistent TCP session with bidirectional transport, does not itself establish an inward command service, application authority or route to RMS. W2 explicitly found no verified reverse ICT-SIEM-to-RMS pivot and noted that a correctly implemented diode would prevent it (`workshops/week-02/adversary-profile.json:6,107`). The new connector premise does not retroactively invalidate that historical negative finding.

## Attack vector

The scenario's entry into plant operations is **misuse of a compromised remediation connector with lower-zone command capability**, followed by RMS staging and unauthenticated Modbus commands to EDGC. It is not a demonstrated exploit of the log stream itself. W1's **Level 3/4 log transfer boundary** and **Level 3/4 boundary firewall** rows identify the architectural risk; the physical map's connector unknown identifies the response-authority risk. The scenario supplies both the reachable path and active response capability that the earlier work could not verify. Sources: `workshops/midterm/scenario.md:3,24-25`; `workshops/week-01/attack-surface-map-it.md:18,32`; `workshops/week-01/attack-surface-map-ot.md:21,36`; `workshops/week-01/attack-surface-map-physical.md:31`.

Credential abuse, transfer over remote services, scripted activity and host evidence removal match the profiled **T0859/T0867/T0886/T0853/T0872** procedures, while the two EDGC commands match **T1692.001**. The match is at the technique level: no WMImplant, PowerShell, RDP, TRITON binary, Triconex controller, credential-capture technique, or program append is identified in these artifacts. W2's historical procedures refer to public TRITON reporting; its profile expressly did not document nuclear/RMS targeting. Sources: `workshops/week-02/adversary-profile.json:22-87,89-107`; `workshops/midterm/scenario.md:3,11-12,24-25`.

At EDGC, the evidence is **accepted unauthorized commands to two different objects**, not an authenticated operator action. The connector's service credentials must not be represented as Modbus writer authentication: the controller journal explicitly labels the writes unauthenticated. W4's backend results show why reachability plus a writable object can matter without a CVE or authenticated human identity, but do not establish this fictional controller's exact server implementation, access-control policy or the actual network route (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:11-12`; `workshops/week-04/adversary-test.md:124-201`; `workshops/week-04/rbac-policy.md:219-247`).

## Physical consequence chain

The causal structure has **two command branches that interact**, not a mask write that itself actuates the inhibit:

| Step | Cyber-to-physical link | Evidence and confidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Unauthorized writes** | **21:40:14Z:** FC06 changes mask register 40009 from 0000 to FFFF. **21:40:15Z:** separate FC05 sets inhibit coil 0004 to 1. | **Recorded (scenario)**, Artifact A, `workshops/midterm/scenario.md:11-12`. Separate objects, functions and seconds; the first write does not establish that it caused the second. |
| **2. Masking plus inhibition** | The mask suppresses fault visibility; the coil is described as blocking EDG cranking on LOOP. At discovery the mask is FFFF and raw fault word is 0008, identified as inhibit active on both trains. | **Recorded/stipulated logical behavior**, Artifacts A/C, `scenario.md:15-17,34`. Strong concern for functional inhibition; not independent mechanical confirmation. |
| **3. Falsely reassuring summary** | Summary remains **1/NORMAL_READY** despite the raw fault: a 04:00Z poll reports normal and the 04:05Z audit exposes the discrepancy. Masking could conceal a readiness deficit from consumers. | **Recorded (scenario)** mismatch/poll and **inferred** concealment consequence, `scenario.md:5,14-17,26`. Exact conversion expression is ambiguous as explained below; no specific operator reliance or delay is proved. |
| **4. Potential failure to crank on LOOP** | **If** the supplied live coil coupling governs the actual train control state and the inhibit remains asserted at a loss-of-offsite-power demand, it would block cranking under Artifact C's described logic. | **Conditional physical consequence**, `scenario.md:5,17,34`. No LOOP signal, start demand, actual failed start or full-window independent availability record is supplied. |
| **5. Conditional emergency-power capability loss** | **If** both credited trains cannot auto-start when needed and other available credited capability does not fulfill the required function, emergency-power capability would be impaired/lost for that function. | **Engineering inference**, not an observed blackout or plant outcome. Credited architecture, redundancy, operating mode and independent operability assessment are missing; `scenario.md:5,35`. Regulatory and LCO determinations are separate in `workshops/midterm/incident-classification.md`. |

**Summary-expression ambiguity:** Artifact A calls 40001 `1 (NORMAL_READY; computed as Raw_Fault_Word & ~Reg_40009)`. With the supplied raw `0x0008` and mask `0xFFFF`, that bitwise expression yields **zero**, not one. It can describe a masked **fault word**, but producing a readiness Boolean of one would require an additional conversion not shown. An inversion or zero-fault comparison is a possible explanation, **not verified code**. Retain the supplied normal-summary/raw-inhibit observations and masking interpretation, but do not silently rewrite the expression or claim the snippet fully verifies implementation. The full conversion, bit widths and deployed mapping need clarification (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:15-17,30-34`).

**Fuel is not the physical failure proof.** The narrative dates a pinned zero value to 21:40, but Artifact A says "Static 0 since service build," and Artifact C says `SIM_FUEL_TANK_IMPLEMENTED = False`, always zero. The zero therefore does not date an attacker-induced fuel change or establish tank depletion. Conversely, that placeholder does not make coil 0004 fictitious or harmless: Artifact C explicitly distinguishes the fuel stub from live auto-start logic. Independent panel/engineering evidence is required to resolve the physical branch, not a cosmetically normal register (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:5,18,33-34`).

## What this analysis cannot establish

1. **Independent attribution or malware identity.** The assignment ties the campaign to the profiled actor/tradecraft; the excerpts do not identify operators, prove all TEMP.Veles/XENOTIME labels identical, or demonstrate TRITON deployment. W2's nuclear application is explicitly hypothetical, distinct from its 2017 historical precedent (`workshops/midterm/README.md:8-14`; `workshops/week-02/adversary-profile.json:11-22,89-107`).
2. **Complete routes, principals or process execution.** Connector service credentials are stipulated, but their identity, compromise method and precise privileges are absent. The outward log session does not explain reverse application command semantics. RMS-to-EDGC flow and EDGC writes lack packet/session/process correlation; no RDP/SSH, domain trust, firewall opening or root escalation is established. The narrative supplies the path, while the excerpts leave those mechanics unknown (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:3,11-12,24-25`; `workshops/week-01/attack-surface-map-ot.md:23,36`).
3. **Continuous physical inoperability or a realized accident.** The two command timestamps and discovery audit do not prove both mechanical trains were incapable throughout the interval. Artifact C strengthens the live-inhibit hypothesis but is not a demand test. No actual LOOP, failed start, blackout, shutdown, radiation release or material diversion is supplied. Intact mechanics also would not by themselves negate logically blocked auto-start (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:5,11-18,33-35`).
4. **A six-hour exposure ending at discovery.** The explicit gap is **2026-10-29T21:40:15Z -> 2026-10-30T03:40:15Z = 6h**. Inhibit-write-to-discovery is **6h24m45s**; mask-write-to-discovery is **6h24m46s**. The post-gap remainder is **24m45s**, with no complete entries supplied. Missing entries do not prove no actions occurred, that inhibition persisted unchanged, or who caused each logging failure; Artifact A names rate limiting and manual flush without a principal. Use the appendix's absolute October 29-30 dates for "yesterday/today," not the conversation date. These are elapsed-time calculations, not legal clocks (`workshops/midterm/scenario.md:5,9-18`).
5. **Historical-to-fictional continuity or expanded plant scope.** W5's **116181** and **116180** are earlier SIEM assertions with unresolved point/material facts, not proof of the new campaign's mechanisms or a compromise of every W5 system. Its coordinator expressly rejects a proved causal chain. Some RPS/PSI rows are secondhand, and Canvas evidence is user-pasted with unavailable originals/permalinks; do not transform those into independent new observations (`workshops/week-05/coordinator-narrative.md:4-8`; `workshops/week-05/three-framework-analysis.json:27-38,51-58`; `workshops/week-05/session-log.md:166-172`).
6. **Current containment, clean recovery or completed coordination.** W4's tested gateway mitigation and W5's dated handover/key/documentation work establish only their own scope/date. They do not prove the fictional connector disabled, EDGC writes blocked, logs restored, trains operable or successor briefed. Current ICT-SIEM ownership and RMS's full/current owner identity remain unconfirmed; W5 supplies only "Tamas, incoming owner," while the scenario names Dominick Wheeler as future EDGC owner (`workshops/week-04/rbac-policy.md:217-295`; `workshops/week-05/three-framework-analysis.json:18-25,56`; `workshops/midterm/scenario.md:5`).

The decisive missing inputs are the scenario authority's full register/source semantics, independent train operability and state history, connector/RMS session and command provenance, and existing acknowledgement/engineering records. They are requests for evidence or clarification, not authorization to connect to plant hosts or run tests. No live-system verification, attack execution, remediation or messages sent are claimed by this analysis.
